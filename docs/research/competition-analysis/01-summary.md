# New Venture competition analysis

Oct 3, 2026 · @Atlas

New Venture's door is not unique today. At least four tools already sell a three-state AI verdict on an idea for USD 5–59, delivered in minutes. AI co-founders and builders start at USD 16–120 a month.

What no product examined does is enforce the rules around the door. None ties its verdict to a real paid-order test with a pass mark fixed in advance. None opens its workspace only on a GO, and none publishes a record that shows whether its verdicts were right.

That is the position to take. Nobody has been shown to pick winners among consumer and software ideas from a description, so the best product is the one whose verdicts can be checked. What a founder cannot get for free is a pass mark set, and held, by someone other than themselves.

By New Venture's own protocol the product is PARKED at 14 of 25, and only if the agent-run door can ship within 30 days. The trigger that revives it is also the fastest test: five of fifty strangers pre-pay EUR 149 within seven days.

"Not found" in this document means not among the products examined; the search for further entrants stopped early.

Verified money in the verdict business is small. The best-evidenced validator has taken USD 29,917 since January 2026 and USD 1,452 in its latest month, by payment-processor data ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/preuve-ai)). The only exit found in the category was USD 150,000, for two products together ([Acquire.com](https://blog.acquire.com/startup-acquisition-episode-88/)).

The money has gone to building instead. Lovable confirmed a USD 13.3bn valuation in August 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/12/lovable-confirms-new-13-3b-valuation-raises-another-400m/)).

Outcomes are poor where a tool starts from a bare idea. Polsia's founder is quoted as saying about 10% of its companies "made at least a dollar", in a competitor's review ([Preuve](https://preuve.ai/blog/polsia-review)). NanoCorp's own changelog showed 206 earning companies out of 30,272 in July 2026 ([NanoCorp](https://www.nanocorp.so/changelogs)).

## What is already a commodity, and what no product examined offers

Most of the machinery New Venture describes can be bought today, usually for less. A free first step, a score with a market scan (USD 29–59), a three-state verdict (USD 5–59), guided stages (USD 39–85 a month), agents that build and launch (from USD 16 a month) and a connector endpoint are all on sale. Hand stages 5 to 8 to those tools; do not compete there.

What is missing elsewhere is the set of rules around that machinery:

| Part of New Venture | Nearest thing elsewhere (3 Oct 2026) | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Five binary kill gates, the failed gate on record | Not in any commercial tool read. Enterprise phase-gate processes use knock-out "must-meet" questions | Unique in this market, with a respected lineage |
| PARKED with a written revival trigger | [IdeaSignal](https://ideasignal.ai/) lists "conditions that would flip the verdict" and has a USD 19-a-month monitor on a waitlist | Nearly matched by one tool. Elsewhere the middle state is a score band |
| KILLED is never re-scored unless a fact changed | The opposite is sold: re-scans that raise the score, and "3 pivot directions" | Unique, and backed by the flattery research |
| One test this week with a pre-committed number | [LEANSpark](https://leanspark.ai/) cycles with success criteria (USD 25–200 a month); a [free toolkit](https://github.com/MaxKmet/idea-validation-agents) whose memo names an experiment and kill criteria; a published self-run [sprint plan](https://getforge.com/blog/forge-validation-sprint/) | Pre-set criteria are not new. Open as a paid-order pass mark that the platform sets, enforces and logs |
| Stage 3: the platform drafts the pre-sale, the founder counts paid orders | Prelaunch deposits from USD 250 a month; page generators at EUR 49 and USD 10 that count sign-ups | Open ground, and the least-served stage in the market |
| A workspace that opens only on a GO and ends in a first paying customer or a documented stop | Every workspace found bills by credits or subscription and lets every idea in. One USD 14.99 report withholds its build notes below a score of 70, by a competitor's reading ([Preuve](https://preuve.ai/compare/verdikt)) | Unique among workspaces, and it costs customers: most ideas will not get in |
| Portfolio rules: one end state, three live, one gets the hours | Portfolio views start at USD 120 per venture a month ([GroundControl](https://togroundcontrol.com/pricing/)); otherwise corporate suites | Unique as rules, but they matter only to serial builders |
| Human review of the verdict | Not found at any price. Humans appear only in investor documents (USD 499) and in coaching | No comparator, and the evidence limits what a reviewer may claim |
| A published, checkable track record (not yet in the concept) | One "89%" claim, with a promised quarterly report that could not be found | Open if built, and it cannot be backdated |

Almost everything open is a rule or a commitment, not technology. As text the rules can be copied in an afternoon, and near-equivalents are already free under open-source licences.

As practice they are hard for the builders to copy: a company that bills on credits or subscriptions earns more when every idea goes through. Pay-per-report validators have no such conflict and are one feature away.

The uncomfortable half is positioning. Seen as "an AI verdict on my idea", New Venture competes with USD 29 and loses. Seen as "an AI workspace that builds my venture", it competes with USD 39 and loses.

A scored door also drifts to the middle. Preuve's own numbers: 17.5% of ideas score go, 76.0% land in caution and 6.4% get a hard no-go ([Preuve](https://preuve.ai/blog/startup-validation-benchmarks-2026)). PARKED must therefore do what a score band cannot: hand over the first test, with the number that would move the verdict.

Competitors in detail goes through New Venture part by part, commodity parts included.

## State of the art means a verdict that can be checked

"Our AI knows which ideas will succeed" is a claim the evidence supports for nobody. What has evidence behind it is a method: a hypothesis, a money test and a threshold set beforehand.

| Finding | Evidence | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Experts cannot rank consumer and software ventures from a written summary | 251 experts rated 537 ventures. Their ratings predicted outcomes in hardware, energy, life sciences and medical devices, not in consumer or software | [Scott, Shu and Lubynsky](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v66y2020i3p1278-1299.html) |
| Investors' scores barely predict returns | Partner scores at one large venture firm correlated 0.1 with eventual returns | [Kerr, Nanda and Rhodes-Kropf](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20358/w20358.pdf) |
| Left alone, a model says yes | An unaided GPT-4o reached 21% precision at 100% recall on 50 startups: "a pronounced over-prediction bias" | [Wang, Ihlamur and Alican](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.19456) |
| Models flatter the user | 11 leading models endorsed the user's position 49% more often than humans did | [Stanford, on a study in Science](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2026/03/ai-advice-sycophantic-models-research) |
| Open-ended AI advice can hurt weaker founders | 640 Kenyan entrepreneurs: no average effect. High performers may have gained over 15%; low performers did nearly 10% worse | [Otis and co-authors](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2024.06909) |
| A testing method does help | Four randomised trials, 759 startups: founders taught to test hypotheses were 9.8 points more likely to stop their project, did so 2.7 weeks earlier, and earned more | [Camuffo and co-authors](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/32437/) |
| The pass mark has to come from outside | In experiments with senior executives, a predetermined decision rule or a fresh decision-maker reduced the pull to continue. Rules people set for themselves did little | [Boulding, Morgan and Staelin](https://doi.org/10.1177/002224379703400114) |
| Money beats words | Stated willingness to pay runs 21% above real. In crowdfunding the number of backers predicts later market performance; the amount raised does not | [Schmidt and Bijmolt](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-019-00666-6), [Stanko and Henard](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/respol/v46y2017i4p784-798.html) |
| A record is provable only going forward | Forecasts are registered before the outcome and every one is scored. Back-tests fail because models have memorised what happened | [ForecastBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19839), [Lopez-Lira, Tang and Zhu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14765) |

One finding points the other way. Judges' ranks in US venture competitions do predict later success, but those judges saw teams pitch in person ([Howell](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23874)). It supports structured judging of people and products, not of an idea on paper.

For the verdict this means four things:

- **Produced.** A fixed rubric describes every score level in words. The judging model is not told whose idea it is, several models from different families score it, and their disagreement is shown. The demand score moves only with behaviour, never with eloquence.
- **Set from outside.** The platform sets the pass mark and a separate evaluator reads the result. The founder does neither.
- **Worded.** A decision about what to do next, plus a forecast on one test: "we put the chance that at least N strangers pay within seven days at about 30%". No "validated" and no accuracy percentage until a record exists.
- **Proven.** Each forecast is frozen and time-stamped before the test runs, and every one is scored. The calibration is published beside two baselines: the base rate, and a plain chatbot given the same idea.

## Ten moves, ranked by judged impact per day of work

The ranking is a judgement, not a measurement. Effort figures are estimates for a solo founder working with AI agents on the existing toolkit.

| # | Move | When | Why | Effort (estimate) | Cheapest test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Make the paid-order test the product, and the verdict its forecast | Now, delivered as a concierge service | No product examined ties a verdict to a pre-sale with a pass mark. Paying backers predict; words do not | About a week | The five-of-fifty pre-sale below |
| 2 | Harden the verdict: worded anchors for every score, a blind judge, three models, and a demand score capped at 2 without behavioural evidence | The anchors and the cap before the pre-sale; the rest before public launch | Flattery and run-to-run inconsistency are measured weaknesses of AI verdicts | 3–5 days | Score 20 ideas with and without ownership cues, five runs each; publish the gap and the spread |
| 3 | Start a public forecast log on your own ventures | Now | No competitor examined publishes a checkable record, and a log cannot be backdated | 1–2 days | 20 resolved forecasts from the weekly tests the protocol already requires |
| 4 | Name the lineage: phase-gate knock-outs, KICKBOX boxes, the pretotyping evidence scale, the test card | Before public launch | Mechanics that enterprises already trust; New Venture uses most of them unnamed | 1–2 days | Use them on the next own-venture test |
| 5 | Sell the human review as an evidence check, not a prediction | With the pre-sale | Experts cannot predict consumer and software ideas. A reviewer can verify the facts and the fairness of the test | 30 minutes per review, capped | Offer it to the first five buyers; keep it if two take it |
| 6 | Reprice around the test and a defined end | With the pre-sale | The draft prices lose against AI anchors and hold against human ones | 1 day | The pre-sale tests the price directly |
| 7 | Cover the EU basics: AI disclosure, withdrawal rights, claims, VAT | Before the pre-sale | The duties apply from the first sale | 1–2 days plus an hour with a lawyer | The lawyer's hour: written answers on consumer status, the AI marking duty and VAT |
| 8 | Run everything hosted and hand out no files | Before any outsider gets access | A plugin installs as files on the user's machine. A private repository controls who clones, not what they keep | 2–6 weeks; concierge delivery until then | Paste the public gate text into a chat assistant for five ideas and compare the output |
| 9 | List the free Gates in assistant directories | After the pre-sale passes | Free discovery. The directories are not stores | Days, once the connector branch is merged | Count gate runs that reach the paid offer |
| 10 | Sell through organisations that already hold idea-stage founders (an untested proposal) | After the pre-sale passes | Distribution is the weakest score, and a one-off EUR 149 sale to consumers is the low-ticket model the protocol warns against | A week of outreach | Offer five programme managers a paid pilot; pass if two say yes |

**Move 1 is the centre.** Sell one thing at the door: a verdict plus this week's paid-order test, set up for the founder. The platform drafts the offer, the messages and the order page, connects payment in the founder's own account, sets the number and logs a forecast. Seven days later it counts paid orders from strangers against that number.

**Move 3 is a head start, not a moat.** A log cannot be backdated, but a rival with 25 buyers a month could fill one in about four months. One test a week on your own ventures takes about two years to reach 96 forecasts, the count that pins a hit rate to within ten points. Twenty forecasts give about 22 points and fifty about 14.

The first public case should be your own. Take the live venture through stage 3 with a logged forecast and publish the result, pass or fail, before selling to strangers.

**Move 8 buys time, not defence.** Prompts leak: in one test, system prompts were extracted from 97.2% of 216 custom GPTs ([Yu and co-authors](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.11538v2)). What survives copying is what accumulates: the outcome dataset, each venture's state and connections, and the public record.

**Move 10 rests on two facts and one assumption.** Selective programmes turn most applicants away: Antler UK backs fewer than 1%, and Y Combinator and Techstars accept between 1% and 3% by a third-party count ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Startup_accelerator)). A university centre has sold feasibility studies since 1980, at USD 980 today ([Wisconsin Innovation Service Center](https://www.uww.edu/wisc)).

The assumption, which nothing in this research tests, is that such organisations would pay for verdicts for the founders they advise or turn away.

## The draft prices against the anchors

&#91;embedded content: vendor pricing pages read 3 October 2026 · 10 price anchors and three draft prices · EUR 1 = USD 1.13\]

EUR 149 is the highest price found for an AI-only verdict, and 48 hours is the slowest delivery: every AI verdict tool read delivers within the hour. EUR 990 per 30 days is about 9 times Cofounder.co's entry plan and about 29 times Buildpad's.

| Tier | Draft | View |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gates | Free, about 15 minutes | Keep. A free first step is universal; founder-answered gates are the distinctive version |
| Verdict | EUR 149, 48 hours | Too expensive and too slow for a desk verdict. It is the right range if the 48 hours end in a live order page and a logged forecast. Give the instant desk verdict away or price it low, and charge for the test |
| Human review | + EUR 200 | A plausible price. Sell it as an evidence check and cap the volume, or gate 4 fails |
| Develop | EUR 990 per 30 days | Programme pricing. Sell a time-boxed sprint with a fee, a deadline and an exit test, not workspace access. No credit meter and no revenue share |
| Run | Included while live | Fine for now |

**What a NO-GO buyer gets.** The gates are free, so nobody pays to be killed at a gate. A paying customer who lands in PARKED receives the test, the number and one re-score when the result is in. That answers most of the open question in the concept.

One case is not covered by the protocol: a paying customer who passes the gates but scores under 13. Decide before the first sale whether that is PARKED with a test, or a refund.

**Outcome-based pricing.** One vendor has cut its revenue share: Polsia went from 20% of revenue to 3% of customer payments within 2026, by its founder's comment and a third-party reading of its terms ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/polsia), [Digitalix Hub](https://www.digitalixhub.com/vs/polsia)). Audos still takes 15% of revenue and NanoCorp 20% of withdrawals.

If an outcome link is wanted, split the Develop fee: part at the start, part when the first stranger's payment lands in the customer's own account. Never promise the outcome itself.

**A smaller first box.** Consider a sprint for stages 3 and 4 only, so a GO customer can buy the pre-sale before committing to the build. What it should cost is for the first buyers to settle, not this research.

**Grants.** Innovation Norway's Oppstartstilskudd 1 pays up to NOK 150,000 for services bought from external suppliers ([Innovation Norway](https://www.innovasjonnorge.no/tjeneste/oppstartstilskudd-1)). It covers only limited companies under five years old on a demanding technology path, and whether a platform fee qualifies is unconfirmed. Treat it as a door-opener for a minority of buyers, not a pricing foundation.

## New Venture on its own protocol: 14 of 25, PARKED

The protocol should apply to its own product. Used inside your own ventures, the backbone is infrastructure and needs no verdict. Sold to strangers, it is a venture like any other.

| Gate | Evidence | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Reach 10 potential buyers this week without anyone's permission | Idea-stage founders gather in open communities, but each community sets its own rules on unsolicited messages. A directory listing needs a review, so it does not count | Pass, untested: name three communities whose rules allow one-to-one messages before the test starts |
| 2. Someone already pays for a worse version | One validator has taken USD 29,917 (payment-verified). Buildpad charges USD 39–85 a month, Founder Institute USD 1,199, a validation studio from USD 4,500 | Pass, with a warning: the desk verdict alone is a saturated commodity |
| 3. Something sellable in 30 days or less of solo work | The concierge version is sellable now, but it is the test rig, not the product. A thin hosted, agent-run door is estimated at 2–6 weeks | Borderline: pass only if the agent-run door ships within 30 days |
| 4. Still earns if you are not delivering each unit | An agent-run verdict and an automatically scored test pass. The concierge version takes one to two hours of your time per verdict, and so does a human review on every verdict | Pass only if agent-run, with the review capped |
| 5. Still willing to work on this in 18 months | Filling the forecast log takes about two years on your own tests alone | Your call |

Read strictly, no version passes gates 3 and 4 together today: the concierge fails gate 4, and the agent-run door is not built. Under the protocol a single NO kills, so the score below is provisional on shipping the agent-run door within 30 days.

| Dimension | Score | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence of demand | 2 | Level 2 on the protocol's scale is "competitor exists". No stranger has paid, and nothing on file shows a named person asking what it would cost |
| Time to first euro | 4 | The concierge version could take money within two to four weeks |
| Price point | 3 | The Verdict is a low-ticket purchase made once or twice. Develop is high-ticket but unvalidated, and sold only to the minority who get a GO |
| Distribution edge | 2 | The audiences on record are in music and apparel, not among idea-stage founders. The real edge is small: three own ventures as public case studies |
| Margin after fulfilment | 3 | Digital and cheap to run, but the draft offer spends founder hours on reviews and check-ins. It rises to 4 or 5 if those are capped |
| **Total** | **14** | **PARKED (13–17)** |

That matches the stated status: to be launched later. A parked idea needs a written revival trigger, and this one writes itself. If strangers pay, evidence of demand goes from 2 to 5 and time to first euro from 4 to 5, which makes 18: a GO.

The total is sensitive to one score. If price point is a 2, because the verdict is a low-ticket sale, the total is 13 today and 17 after a pass: still PARKED.

Two scores are yours to correct. Demand rises to 3 or 4 if named people have already described the problem or asked for the price. Distribution rises if you hold a founder audience this research could not see; neither correction alone reaches 18.

One more rule applies after a pass. Three ventures already sit on the backbone and live ventures are capped at three. New Venture goes live as a product only when one of them is parked or killed.

## The fastest test: five of fifty strangers pay within seven days

The test needs no new build, but the EU basics in move 7 come first, and so does a named seller.

| Element | Specification |
| --- | --- |
| Hypothesis | At least 10% of idea-stage founders who face a real decision will pay EUR 149 up front for a verdict plus a money test that is set up for them |
| Offer | Free Gates first. Then "Verdict plus this week's test": the venture card, one test with its number, a drafted offer and order page, and the result scored a week later. EUR 149 including VAT, delivered within 48 hours by you running the existing toolkit |
| Who counts | Fifty people who have never met you, each with a specific idea and a decision involving money or time in the next 30 days |
| Channel | One-to-one messages in founder communities whose rules allow them, ten a day for five working days. No friends and no existing followers |
| Payment | A payment link, paid before delivery. Consent to immediate delivery captured at checkout; refund on request within 14 days |
| **Pre-committed number** | **PASS: 5 or more paid orders by day 7 that are still unrefunded on day 21. FAIL: 0 or 1. Between 2 and 4: stays PARKED, with one re-run in which exactly one thing changes (the price or the audience) and the number does not** |
| Before it starts | Move 7: one to two days plus an hour with a lawyer, and a named selling entity. A merchant-of-record checkout can collect the VAT on these orders; check first that its terms cover a service like this |
| Cost | About five hours of outreach and one to two hours per verdict delivered. No platform build |

The number is a proposal from outside, which is the point: rules people set for themselves do little. Write it down before the first message goes out, and do not change it afterwards.

**Why five of fifty.** Your concept says the first five paying customers set the real prices. Ten per cent is also well above the 1–5% visitor-to-paid conversion reported for a much cheaper validator in 2023 ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/concept-to-cash-6-figure-exit-in-6-months-for-dimeadozen-ai-2b557c6205)). A direct message to a qualified person is not a landing-page visit, so that comparison is rough.

**Read the result with care.** Fifty messages separate only large differences. At a true rate of 10% the test passes about 57% of the time and fails about 3%. At 4% it passes about 5% and fails about 40%.

A pass is therefore strong evidence. A fail rules out 10%, not 4%, so decide before the test which rate the business needs. Log the funnel step by step: messages sent, replies, Gates completed, orders paid.

**What a pass is worth.** Five orders bring EUR 745 including VAT for 10–15 hours of your time. At the best-evidenced rival's volume, about 25 reports a month, the Verdict would gross about EUR 3,700 a month. The verdict is the door, not the business.

The money has to come from Develop sprints or from organisations, and neither has a price test yet. Set a second number before the pre-sale: how many GO buyers must pay a deposit on a sprint.

**Four readings come free.** How many buyers send their own pre-sale within seven days, and how many take the human review. Also who asks for a refund, after which verdict, and what each buyer says made them pay. Every verdict delivered yields a logged forecast, so a pass starts the public record.

**A fail has a narrow meaning.** It kills "one order per ten cold messages at EUR 149", with the count and the date on record. It does not test a lower rate or another channel, and the backbone stays untouched as your internal system.

**One conflict with the portfolio rules.** The protocol allows one test a week, on the single venture that gets the hours, and this pre-sale spends that slot for a week. Two steps cost nothing in the meantime: start the forecast log on the tests the live venture already runs, and write the revival trigger into New Venture's card today.

## Five threats, and three results that would prove this plan wrong

| Threat | Evidence | Defence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The platforms absorb the path | Deep research sits in USD 20 plans. Shopify's [connector in Claude](https://claude.com/connectors/shopify) creates a store from a description. Anthropic ships a small-business package, and OpenAI a small-business programme with a general work agent | Hold the ground they have not taken: a committed go or kill, and responsibility for the first customer. Hand stages 5 to 8 to their tools |
| Commoditisation | Verdicts cost USD 5, and are free in chat and as open-source skills. Near neighbours are one feature away: a flip-condition monitor on a waitlist, a USD 149 launch kit | The record and the dataset, which take time to build |
| Low outcome rates | About 10% of Polsia's companies made a dollar, by its founder's account ([Preuve](https://preuve.ai/blog/polsia-review)). 67.5% of revenue-disclosing indie products sit in the USD 0–1,000 bracket; monthly or total is not stated ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/stats)) | Time-boxed fees survive this better than subscriptions. Sell the documented stop as a result: the decision reached, the money not spent |
| Founders ignore or resent a kill | Negative feedback raised abandonment by 13% in venture competitions, a modest effect ([Howell](https://doi.org/10.1093/rof/rfab006)). People rate flattering AI as higher quality ([Cheng and co-authors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01395)) | Keep kills in the free step, frame the paid result as a test, and sell to people with a decision at stake |
| Rules shift | AI Act transparency duties have applied since 2 August 2026 ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)). ChatGPT's app rules bar selling digital services, upsells included ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/app-submission-guidelines)). GummySearch, a research tool used for validation, closed in November 2025 ([GummySearch](https://gummysearch.com)) | An hour with a lawyer before launch, payment on your own site, and no dependence on one data source |

The plan in this document is wrong if any of three things happens:

1. Buyers pay for the verdict but do not run the test. Then there is no record, and no difference from a USD 29 report.
2. Founder-sent tests reach mostly friends and existing followers. Then passes are inflated and the record means little.
3. The paying market proves too thin. EU business registrations fell in the first two quarters of 2026, while US business applications ran about 17% above 2025 for January to August ([Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Quarterly_registrations_of_new_businesses_and_declarations_of_bankruptcies_-_statistics), [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/BABATOTALSAUS.txt)).

Each has a cheap test in the table of assumptions below.

## Assumptions, and the cheapest test for each

The facts this document leans on are listed with their reliability in Sources and open items. These are the assumptions it rests on:

## How this was researched and checked

Eight research strands read vendor pages, pricing pages, papers and official sources on 3 October 2026. Seven independent checks then re-opened 445 claims against their pages: 366 were confirmed, 18 of them with a caveat, and 79 needed correcting, softening or removing. A final read-through caught the last of those and tested the reasoning; its corrections are in this version.

Three limits matter. The web-search allowance ran out part-way, so "not found" means "not among the products examined". Reddit could not be read, which is the largest hole in the sentiment evidence. No competitor reports were bought and no idea was entered into any tool, so competitors' full reports are judged from their sales pages and samples.

The legal points summarise public sources and are not legal advice.

The detail sits in four tabs:

- Competitors in detail holds the six kinds of competitor, with prices, verdicts, tests and traction for each product.
- Evidence holds the studies behind the state-of-the-art argument, with their limits.
- Moves in detail holds each move in full: what to do, the evidence and the test.
- Sources and open items holds the facts with their reliability, what remains unverified or conflicting, and the main sources.
