# Competitors in detail

Six kinds of product compete for a person with an idea. Every price was read on 3 October 2026 unless another date is given, and is shown in the vendor's own currency.

Traction is labelled by reliability. **Verified** means a payment processor, a review platform's own counter or an official page. **Vendor-claimed** means the company's own number, unaudited. **Third-party** means press, analysts or, often, a competitor.

## AI idea validators: a three-state verdict is now a commodity

The category is crowded and cheap to enter. Of 21 names on the starting list, 17 are live, and at least ten new pay-per-idea tools appeared in 2025–2026. The standard product takes a paragraph and returns a scored report in one to ten minutes.

Across 13 one-time prices verified on vendor pages, the median is USD 14.99 and the mean about USD 29. The only AI report at USD 100 or more is DimeADozen at USD 129, whose paid report offers evidence in place of a go/no-go: "the evidence to answer it yourself" ([DimeADozen pricing](https://www.dimeadozen.ai/pricing)).

Three facts in this table matter for New Venture:

- **Verified money is tiny.** The best-evidenced tool, with live-source research and nine months in market, took USD 1,452 in its latest month and about USD 3,300 a month on average since launch. The only documented exit in the category is USD 150,000. Pay-per-report validation, sold alone, has so far been a small business.
- **No vendor contacts a buyer.** Vendors compete on citation counts, which measure desk research, not demand. Two hand the founder a waitlist page and outreach messages, with no pass mark.
- **Scores drift upward when ideas are re-described.** Preuve reports that "rescanned ideas gain +8.9 points on average" and offers three pivots to improve the score ([Preuve](https://preuve.ai)).

The known weak spots are flattery, arbitrary scores and invented numbers. In one Hacker News thread from August 2025, four users reported a score of 75 out of 100 from a validator, one of them for every idea he typed; five others got scores from 25 to 65. One found that an idea "Like Uber, except for beekeepers" was given "good potential" with an obtainable market of USD 500 million a year within five years ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44881440)).

The only accuracy claim among the validators read, IdeaProof's 89%, comes without a cohort, a sample size or a description of how survival was checked, according to a competitor's review ([Preuve on IdeaProof](https://preuve.ai/compare/ideaproof)).

Top-end prices are unsettled. DimeADozen's full report was USD 39 in 2023 and is USD 129 now, although it added a USD 9 entry tier ([AI World Today](https://www.aiworldtoday.net/p/how-two-friends-used-ai-to-build-startup)). Preuve's own page shows USD 59, while its payment-verified listing and a competitor's comparison show USD 29, and no page states a change ([IdeaProof vs Preuve](https://ideaproof.io/preuve-ai-vs-ideaproof)). There are buyers at higher prices, but not many of them.

The money has gone to building, not to venture guidance. Lovable was reported at a USD 500M run rate in June 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/12/lovable-confirms-new-13-3b-valuation-raises-another-400m/)). The largest self-described co-founder tool was built by two founders who worked alone for almost two years and are hiring a team in 2026 ([Buildpad](https://buildpad.io/our-story)).

The outcome data is the important part. In the published cases the tools worked where demand existed before the build: Lovable's documented successes had clients or an audience lined up, according to a competitor's review ([Preuve](https://preuve.ai/blog/lovable-review)). Where the tool started from a bare idea, almost nothing earned money.

Polsia's own data in September 2026 showed 29,132 active companies out of 484,592 created. About 41% of its headline run rate was non-recurring: credit packs, pass-through ad spend, domains and its users' own revenue (a competitor's reading of vendor data: [Preuve](https://preuve.ai/blog/polsia-review)).

The best published first-revenue figures belong to a human accountability club with no AI at all. Ship or Die members shipped 155 startups in the first 30 days and 57 of them made revenue, with 291 founders aboard by July 2026 (a competitor's review that names no source: [Preuve](https://preuve.ai/blog/ship-or-die-review)).

The complaints are consistent across builders: credits burned on the tool's own mistakes, and lock-in. A Base44 reviewer quotes support as saying "our policy is that credits are non-refundable for AI behavior, even when the results are frustrating" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/base44.com)).

At Polsia, customer payments sit in a Polsia balance before they reach the customer ([Preuve](https://preuve.ai/blog/polsia-review)). A rival builder's review answers "who owns the code and infrastructure" with "Polsia, not you" ([superapp.dev](https://superapp.dev/blog/polsia-review-2026-is-it-legit-pricing-real-reviews)). Both sources compete with Polsia.

Two things follow for New Venture. A fixed fee with no credit meter and customer-owned accounts is a visible contrast. And stages 5 to 8 of the platform (build, page, launch, automate) are the most commoditised work in the market; a premium for them is unlikely.

The large AI labs have not shipped a "start a business" product. Anthropic's small-business package of 13 May 2026 is fifteen workflows and fifteen skills for an existing business ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-for-small-business)); the announcement also backs a small accelerator for 15 aspiring solo founders, which is a programme, not a product. OpenAI ships training plus a general work agent ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-small-business-program/)), and xAI ships general "AI teammates" ([xAI](https://x.ai/bot)).

The idea-to-first-customer path is left to third parties, for now.

Selective programmes turn most applicants away: Antler UK backs fewer than 1%, and Y Combinator and Techstars accept between 1% and 3% by a third-party count. As far as this research found, rejected applicants get no written reason. Studio-grade gating is not for sale to someone with their own idea: where outside ideas are accepted, the founder passes selection and gives up equity. A fee-based service that applies the same gates to the founder's own idea has no direct human-led equivalent in this set.

The open, cheap layer is also thin and unstable. Buildspace closed in August 2024 with more than two years of runway left, Pioneer stopped investing in 2024, and Small Bets is closed to new members ([Open Almanac](https://www.openalmanac.org/w/founders-inc/the-night-buildspace-closed), [Pioneer](https://pioneer.app/), [Small Bets](https://smallbets.com/)).

One useful model sits here. Pioneer Square Labs published a kill memo for a music-lesson marketplace: the tests run, the click-through and conversion rates obtained, the projected revenue of USD 21,600 against about USD 80,000 of acquisition cost, and the date ([PSL kill memo](https://www.psl.com/feed-posts/psl-studio-kill-xylo)). That is what "the reason written down" looks like when it is done well.

## Free and open-source substitutes set the price floor at zero

A subscription of about USD 20 a month now includes web research and task-running agents. Claude Pro lists "Research" and "Hand off and schedule tasks"; ChatGPT Plus lists "Expanded deep research" and its Work agent ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing), [ChatGPT pricing](https://chatgpt.com/pricing/), [ChatGPT Plus](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6950777-what-is-chatgpt-plus)). The marginal cost of one more idea is close to zero, and on GitHub the verdict pattern is already free.

Two readings of this table are both true. A scored verdict with staged gates is neither novel nor protectable as an idea. Yet build toolkits draw tens to hundreds of thousands of stars (gstack about 135,000) while validation toolkits stay under 1,000, so the free substitute is real but pulls weakly on the target customer.

The documented weakness of the do-it-yourself route is reliability, not capability. One 2026 study measured only 39–77% factual accuracy behind valid-looking citations for the strongest models in deep-research output, and as low as 24% across all 14 models tested ([arXiv 2605.06635](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06635v1)). Another found 3–13% fabricated URLs ([arXiv 2604.03173](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03173v1)).

Those are properties of the same models New Venture runs on.

The tooling is not the barrier: a founder can assemble an interest test for under USD 50. Traffic, a fair pass mark and an honest reading are the barrier, and that is the service.

## New Venture part by part

The Summary lists what no product examined offers. This table goes through the whole concept, commodity parts included.
